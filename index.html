My engineering work sits at the point where biosignals meet interface design — reading a hand gesture, an EMG pulse, or a sensor stream, and turning it into something a clinician or patient can act on without friction. That thread runs from an EMG-driven prosthetic finger through a gesture-controlled DICOM viewer, to my latest work: a browser-native pipeline for visualizing patient-specific coronary anatomy on consumer hardware, heading to IEEE TENCON 2026 in Bali.

Alongside the lab work, I've spent three years growing IEEE's presence at KPRIET — from writing my first newsletter as a first-year Content Writer to now chairing a student branch that runs 24 technical societies and affinity groups, while representing the Madras Section at IEEE FLEPS (Singapore) and IEEE EMBC (Toronto).
